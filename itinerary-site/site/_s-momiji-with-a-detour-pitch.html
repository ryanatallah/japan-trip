This is the fork you liked, moved a fortnight earlier so it clears Thanksgiving — home on 22 November, three days before you fly to Colorado. Everything you actually named survives: Gora Kadan, Kikunoi, Jugyuan, the sword smithing, the deep kintsugi, the flying squirrels, and three nights at a Relais & Châteaux ryokan at 1,000m in the Northern Alps.

What changes is the Kyoto strategy. On 17–21 November you are ahead of the central temples but right on top of the northern ones — Takao, Kurama and Kibune, Ohara, Rurikoin all colour one to two weeks before the city floor. So the week points north and uphill instead of doing the Higashiyama postcard tour. The Alps stop gains too: a fortnight earlier means Kamikochi and the Venus Line are still open, which they are not on the original dates.

Rail

Still entirely on rails, with two new legs through Nagoya. Odawara→Nagoya→Matsumoto is about 3h20; Matsumoto→Nagoya→Kyoto about 3h. Both are a shinkansen plus the Limited Express Shinano, which runs roughly hourly through the Kiso valley — a genuinely scenic ride rather than dead time. Takkyubin the big bags Hakone→Kyoto and travel the Alps leg light.
