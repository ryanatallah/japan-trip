Or swap the Alps for the coast. The other Relais & Châteaux option you raised, Beniya Mukayu in Kaga, is below — and on paper it beats Matsumoto for late November: snow crab season is open, Kenrokuen is 25 minutes away, and its kitchen is already documented as gluten-free capable. It is also two hours from Kyoto rather than three. The reason it is not the default here is that Itinerary 2 already spends three nights in exactly that place, so choosing it makes this variant and Itinerary 2 substantially the same trip.

The verdict

The honest ledger. You gain: a Relais & Châteaux ryokan in a landscape nothing else on this trip visits, a National Treasure castle, about $1,200, and — the real prize — a Kyoto block that sits entirely inside the forecast foliage peak instead of straddling it.

You lose: the Nara day, the Ohara day, and the spare Kyoto day that absorbed bad weather and let you go back to something you liked. Five nights in Kyoto at peak is enough for Kikunoi, Jugyuan, the kintsugi, Arashiyama and the big three illuminations, but it is full — there is no slack in it. And late November is the wrong season for the Alps: Kamikochi and the high roads are shut, so the mountains are scenery rather than terrain. If that second point bothers you, the Kaga swap below fixes it, at the cost of overlapping Itinerary 2.
