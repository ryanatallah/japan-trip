How to choose

  • You want the most time in KyotoItinerary 1 — The Kyoto ClassicEight nights across the turn — long enough to repeat a favourite and to lose a day to rain without it mattering. $32.8k
  • You want Kyoto plus one more placeItinerary 1B — The Classic, with the AlpsThree nights at a Relais & Châteaux ryokan in the Alps, and the earlier dates reopen Kamikochi and the Venus Line. $32.3k
  • You want to finish somewhere quietItinerary 1C — The Classic, then the Crab CoastStraight past Kyoto to a seven-generation ryokan on the crab coast, in crab season. The shortest and earliest Kyoto block, so it leans on the northern hills. $32.6k
  • You want the most wildlife for the moneyItinerary 2 — Crafts, Crab & Snow MonkeysFlying squirrels and two macaque troops on one rail line, crab week, and Kenrokuen at its mid-November peak — the one itinerary whose season the re-date actually suits. $29.7k
  • It has to be the Ezo momonga specificallyItinerary 3 — Wildlife NorthThe only route that shows you that species — but the re-date puts you in Hokkaido before the cranes gather, so it is now the squirrel alone. $29.7k
  • Warmth, a road trip and the lowest numberItinerary 4 — Southern WarmthCheapest at $28.2k, the only all-temp-band option, and the biggest buffer before Colorado — but no Tokyo, so no sword smithing, owl café or Karuizawa squirrels
  • Breadth over depthItinerary 5 — The Grand TourThe most places in one trip, at $35.5k, landing on a Thursday. With peak Kyoto gone and Hokkaido now visited early, it is the broad option rather than the best-at-anything one
