Dated around Thanksgiving

Every itinerary below is dated to get you home before Colorado. You fly from California on Wed 25 November and are back on Sat 5 December, so each trip returns on Sun 22 November at the latest — three clear days of buffer, or more.

That has one consequence worth understanding rather than glossing over. Kyoto's 2026 foliage is forecast to peak 25 November – 7 December, which is your Colorado window almost exactly, so nobody is getting central Kyoto at its absolute crest this year. But foliage is a gradient, not a switch: Kyoto colours by elevation, and the northern, higher districts — Takao, Kurama, Kibune, Ohara, Rurikoin — run one to two weeks ahead of the city floor. Kyoto City's own tourism pages put Takao at mid-November. A trip ending 22 November lands on those at their best, while Tofuku-ji and Eikandō are part-turned. The itineraries that spend real time in Kyoto now point north because of it.

Why the dates matter

Kyoto's 2026 foliage is forecast to peak Nov 25 – Dec 7, densest Nov 29 – Dec 3 — later than average. So itineraries wanting peak Kyoto should end as late as possible, with Kyoto last. Kenrokuen in Kanazawa peaks mid-November; the Japan Alps peaked in mid-October and Kamikochi closes Nov 15, so it is excluded; Kyushu runs early to late November; Hokkaido's foliage is already over — there it is pre-winter wildlife season instead. Crane feeding at Tsurui starts mid-to-late November; Ezo momonga are viewable year-round with a guide. Kyoto crowds are worst Nov 15–25, including the Nov 21–23 holiday weekend; the Nov 29 – Dec 3 tail has peak colour with the crowd wave passing.

The trips

Itinerary 1B The plan

The Classic, with the Alps

Fifteen nights — Tokyo, Hakone at its own peak, a Relais & Châteaux ryokan at 1,000m in the Northern Alps, and a Kyoto week pointed north at the early colour

Fri Nov 6 → Sun Nov 22, 2026·15 nights·40–60°F — the Alps stop is the cold part

Tokyo · 4n→Hakone · 3n→Tobira Onsen · 3n→Kyoto · 5n

Fifteen nights, four stops, no car, and everything you named still in it: Gora Kadan, Kikunoi Honten, Kodaiji Jugyuan, the Hirata sword smithing, the deep kintsugi with Mio Heki, the flying squirrels at Karuizawa — plus three nights at a Relais & Châteaux ryokan at 1,000m in the Northern Alps. Out of SFO on Friday 6 November, home from Osaka on Sunday the 22nd, three clear days before Colorado.

The Kyoto week points north and uphill. On 17–21 November you are ahead of the central temples but right on top of the northern ones — Takao, Kurama and Kibune, Ohara and Rurikoin all colour one to two weeks before the city floor. So the week is Takao, Ohara, Kurama and Arashiyama rather than the Higashiyama postcard tour, which is also where most of the crowd is not.

Hakone gets three nights, not two. Two buys exactly one full day, and that day is full before you reach the ropeway. Three gives you the loop day and the art day, two mornings at Lake Ashi instead of one, and arrival on 11 November — dead centre of Hakone's own foliage peak.

And the Alps stop is open. These dates land before the Venus Line shuts on 20 November, and the Shinhotaka Ropeway runs year-round to 2,150m.

Itinerary 1

The Kyoto Classic

The seeds trip, with the longest Kyoto block of any option

Sat Nov 7 → Sun Nov 22, 2026·14 nights·45–65°F

Tokyo · 4n→Hakone · 2n→Kyoto · 8n

Still the trip your seeds describe most literally — Gora Kadan, Kikunoi, Jugyuan, sword smithing, the deep kintsugi, kyudo — with a full eight nights in Kyoto — the longest run in any version. Trimming the workshops did two things: it took $4,000 off the number, and it opened four days that are now the best wildlife in the plan outside Hokkaido. Flying squirrels at Karuizawa on the Wednesday, the Nara deer on the holiday Monday, the Iwatayama macaques above Arashiyama, and an owl café on the first Tokyo afternoon.

The Classic, then the Crab Coast

Four nights in Kyoto, then keep going west to the crab coast

Sat Nov 7 → Sun Nov 22, 2026·14 nights·42–58°F — the coast is the cold end

Tokyo · 4n→Hakone · 2n→Kyoto · 4n→Kinosaki · 4n

Kyoto cut to four nights, then straight past it to the Sea of Japan: Nishimuraya Honkan, a Relais & Châteaux ryokan seven generations deep, in a canal town where you walk between the public bathhouses in a yukata. The trip ends in an onsen town instead of a city.

Itinerary 2

Crafts, Crab & Snow Monkeys

Two wildlife days on one rail line, crab week, and Kenrokuen at peak

Sat Nov 7 → Sun Nov 22, 2026·14 nights·Mostly good; Nagano in the 40s

Tokyo · 3n→Shibu Onsen · 2n→Kanazawa · 2n→Kaga · 2n→Kyoto · 5n

Four regions, the best train seat in Japan, and a set of alignments that only work in this exact fortnight: Kenrokuen at its mid-November peak and snow crab season opening the week before you arrive. Trimming the craft workshops changed what this trip is about — it used to be the densest craft run of the set, and now its real distinction is wildlife. The Hokuriku line happens to thread both Karuizawa and Nagano, so the flying squirrels and the snow monkeys sit two days apart on the same rails. The trade is Kyoto — you arrive Nov 17–22, colour building but likely just before the forecast peak, in exchange for rooms about $300/night cheaper.

Start in Kyushu at 65°F, drive a Lexus across one of the largest volcanic calderas on earth, sleep in a villa with a private hot spring in the woods at Kurokawa, fly to a subtropical island of thousand-year-old cedars, and finish with six nights in Kyoto at pre-peak prices. Itinerary 5

The Grand Tour

The stretch. Everything, at the cost of both budget rules

Sat Oct 31 → Thu Nov 19, 2026·20 days / 18 nights·Mixed

Tokyo · 3n→E. Hokkaido · 4n→Kyoto · 7n→Hakone · 2n→Tokyo · 2n

It is still the only single-trip answer to "Hokkaido plus Kyoto plus Gora Kadan." Flights from SFO

≈ $9,000 – $11,000 per couple, business, round trip

  • Best product: ANA "The Room" on SFO→HND — a 28-inch-wide lie-flat with a closing door. SFO frequencies alternate between the new and old configurations, so verify the seat map before ticketing. About $4,500–5,500 per person booked 3–6 months out.
  • Open-jaw into Tokyo, out of Osaka. Itineraries 1, 3 and 4 end near Kyoto. United is the only SFO–KIX nonstop, and a single United multi-city ticket (SFO→HND / KIX→SFO, Polaris) prices within about $0–500 of a normal round trip. It saves a half-day backtrack to Tokyo.
  • JAL from SFO still flies its older Sky Suite — the new A350 suite is not on SFO yet. Consolidator fares run $3,400–4,800 round trip if you want to shave $2,000.
  • Depart SFO Saturday morning, land in Japan Sunday afternoon. Return flights land at SFO the same day they leave Japan, so a Sunday departure from Japan is a Sunday arrival home.

The gluten-free playbook

For Ashly. This drives more of the restaurant selection than the star counts do.

  • Kikunoi Honten has a formal gluten-free intake on its reservation form. Declare it there — no same-day dietary requests. L'Effervescence prints a personalised GF menu, but it is closed Sundays and Mondays, and dinner-only on Tuesdays and Wednesdays — which decides for you which night of a Tokyo block it can take. Sézanne explicitly refuses gluten-free — avoid it. Monk is pizza-centric — skip.
  • Gora Kadan is two answers, not one — and you need the right one in writing. Its public kaiseki-restaurant page states flatly that "Vegan/Gluten-Free/Halal options are not available," and that page governs the à la carte restaurant, which sells priced courses to day visitors. For staying guests who declare at reservation, the record is the opposite and it is excellent: multiple independent celiac reviews describe full gluten-free kaiseki and breakfast, staff confirming each dish as it is set down, even gluten-free afternoon snacks. Book as a staying guest, declare in writing, and get a written reply. Beniya Mukayu, Asaba and Sankara Yakushima all accommodate with advance notice; Sankara publishes its gluten-free options outright.
  • Tactics: tamari instead of shoyu (request at booking, carry travel packets), yakitori shio not tare, teppanyaki salt-grilled, sushi minus nikiri and marinated pieces. Never tempura, ramen, udon or unagi. Carry a Japanese-language celiac card (Legal Nomads).
  • On multi-night ryokan stays, ask about the alternative courses specifically. Kitchens vary the menu across consecutive nights so you are not eating the same kaiseki twice — but the substitutes are not equally safe. Charcoal-grilled beef is salt-grilled and fine; sukiyaki warishita and shabu-shabu ponzu are both soy-based and are the two worst formats on any ryokan menu. They can be rebuilt with tamari, but only as an explicit request. Confirm each format, not just "gluten-free" in the abstract.
  • Backup restaurants: Gluten Free T's Kitchen in Tokyo (certified GF — they even do GF tempura) and Little Bird in Kyoto.
  • Send every ryokan and restaurant the gluten-free brief 4–6 weeks ahead. A detailed reply is the signal that the kitchen can be trusted — a vague one is a warning.
