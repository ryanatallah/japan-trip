The four bases · 15 nights

Where you sleep, and how far everything is

Bearing is true and distance is time. Each stop sits at its real compass direction from the hotel, but its distance from the centre is door-to-door travel minutes on a log scale — so a three-minute walk and a two-hour day trip can share one figure. It is the question you actually ask standing in a lobby, and a true-scale map cannot answer it: on one of Tokyo, Karuizawa is 140km out and everything in the city collapses to a dot.

Tokyo

Aman Tokyo·Otemachi — floors 33 to 38 of Otemachi Tower·Sat 7 – Tue 10 November · 4 nights

Check in 15:00 · check out 12:00 confirm · see the hotel →

Otemachi is the quietest possible base for this particular week, because three of the four days leave town. It sits on top of five subway lines and is an eight-minute walk from Tokyo Station, which is where both the Ome run and the Karuizawa run start. The cost of that is distance from the nightlife districts — Nishi-Azabu is a taxi, not a walk.

Okunikko

The Ritz-Carlton, Nikko·2482 Chugushi — the north shore of Lake Chuzenji, 1,269m·Wed 11 – Sun 15 November · 4 nights

Check in 15:00 · check out 12:00 confirm · see the hotel →

The governing fact here is daylight, not distance. On 13 November the sun rises at 06:16 and sets at 16:36 — and every paid site in the shrine precinct is shut by 16:00, Shinkyo by 15:30. So the whole of your golden hour happens after you have been locked out of everything you paid for. The schedule that works inverts the usual one: Rinnoji opens at 08:00 and is the only World Heritage site you can enter in raking light; Toshogu opens at 09:00, not the 08:00 the older guidebooks say; and the last ninety minutes of the day belong to Kanmangafuchi and Shinkyo, which are free and never close.

The second fact is elevation. You sleep at 1,269m where the colour finished in October, and the colour you came for is six hundred metres downhill. That forty-minute bus is not a chore on this trip — it is the sightseeing. Drive it rather than bus it: two hundred minutes of a ten-hour day is a lot to give away against unforgiving last-entry times.

  1. 1

    Futarasan Chugushi 5 min on foot confirm

    The shrine is 2484 Chugushi; the hotel is 2482 — adjacent lot numbersHours 09:00–16:00 in November · open year-round · freeThe best low-effort thing available from the door: a mid-mountain shrine founded in 784 at the foot of Nantai, with seven Important Cultural Properties and a thousand-year-old yew. Walk up before breakfast — frost, the lake, and the mountain catching first light, with essentially nobody there.

    Hours Climbing season 25 April – 11 November · registration 06:00–12:00 onlyThe gate is locked from the 12th, so this is off the table. Far quieter than the shrines. Allow ninety minutes.

    Rinnoji — Sanbutsudo, Taiyuin, Shoyoen 40 min car

    Hours 08:00–16:00 in November, last entry 15:30 · the ¥1,000 three-facility set covers all threeThe set-ticket counter closes at 13:30 in November. Shuzenji

Asaba·Shuzenji Onsen, on the Katsura river — Izu peninsula, Shizuoka·Sun 15 – Tue 17 November · 2 nights

Check in 14:30 · check out 11:30 · see the hotel →

The most walkable base of the four, and the one that splits hardest in two. Everything in the old onsen town is inside ten minutes on foot — the temple, the bamboo path, all five bridges, the sutra hall — and the bamboo path is lit until 23:00 with a cut-paper projection thrown onto its round bench from 20:00, which makes the evening walk free and about forty minutes. Everything else needs the car. The wasabi terraces, the Fuji viewpoints and Joren Falls are 25 to 60 minutes away with sparse buses, which is why the rental is picked up at Mishima on the way in rather than argued about later. Check-in closes at 18:00, so the arrival day is not as elastic as it looks.

    Tokko-no-yu 2 min on foot

    Hours Free, always open, lit after darkViewing only — bathing is prohibited and it is not a foot bath either. It lost its legal status as a bathhouse. The actual free foot baths are Kawara-yu and Sugi-no-yu on the opposite bank.

Kyoto

HOTEL THE MITSUI KYOTO·Nakagyo-ku — Aburanokoji at Nijo, facing the castle·Tue 17 – Sun 22 November · 5 nights

Check in 15:00 · check out 12:00 · see the hotel →

This base moved three kilometres west, and that single fact reshapes the week. The old one was in Gion, where both marquee dinners, the kintsugi and the Kodai-ji illumination were all a five-minute walk from bed. From here they are half an hour by subway or a fifteen-minute taxi. What you get back is everything on the other side of the compass: Nijo Castle is across the street, Takao is forty minutes better, Arashiyama is fifteen, Kyoto Station is closer than it was from Gion, and two Michelin restaurants are inside a ten-minute walk. The hinge is Nijojo-mae, three minutes away on the Tozai line — which is really the Higashiyama subway: Sanjo Keihan in five minutes, Higashiyama in seven, Keage in nine, no transfers. And after three cold mountain days in a row, the hot spring in the basement is a better recovery base than a machiya was.
